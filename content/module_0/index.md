# Module 0 — Your Vote and the Map

Somewhere on the map of Ireland there is a small patch — a few thousand people —
that the State treats as a single, unbreakable unit whenever it builds your
constituency. You live in one of several thousand of them, and you have almost
certainly never heard yours named. It is called your Electoral Division, and it
is where the whole story of redistricting begins. Group these patches one way
and your vote sits in a crowded constituency where each TD speaks for more
people than average; group them another way and it does not. You did not pick
the grouping. This module is about who did, and how.

It is written for anyone who votes in Ireland and has never had a reason to look
behind the ballot. It assumes no maths and no prior knowledge of the electoral
system. By the time you finish, you will be able to find the Electoral Division
you live in and see how it is bundled into a constituency, explain why two
identical votes can end up carrying slightly different weight, and judge for
yourself how likely your own area is to be redrawn at the next review.

Three short lessons, about 30 to 45 minutes in total, and best read in order —
each one builds on the last:

- **[Why This Matters](why_this_matters.md)** — how identical ballots come to
  carry different weight, worked through with real figures from the 2023 review.
- **[Find Your ED](find_your_ed.md)** — locate your own Electoral Division, the
  building block every constituency is assembled from, on an interactive map.
- **[Boundaries and You](boundaries_and_you.md)** — what triggers a boundary
  review, what the Electoral Commission is trying to balance when it redraws the
  map, and how exposed your area is to the next change.

Start with [Why This Matters](why_this_matters.md). When you reach the end of
the module, the next one takes up the mechanics the ballot itself hides — how a
ranked vote actually becomes three, four, or five TDs.
